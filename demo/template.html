UNCERTAFACE

Predict a face from part of a skull, and say how sure the model is, in millimetres.

Read this first. The skulls are real, anonymised CT scans. The faces are synthetic: an assumed layer of soft tissue, not measured faces. This is not a forensic tool and it does not identify anyone.

How it works

1A real skull, partly hidden

Each training example hides 10 to 85 percent of the bone with realistic patterns: patches, a cap, only the top, only one side, or whole regions. The hidden fraction is measured on the surface, not assumed.

2Predict a face

A point-cloud network reads the visible bone and predicts the position of about 600 face points. A fixed template gives every skull the same vertex layout.

3Say how unsure

For every point the model also predicts a spread in millimetres. That spread is then checked and corrected on separate skulls using conformal calibration.

4Check against the truth

The synthetic face is known exactly, so error and uncertainty can be compared point by point on skulls kept out of training.

What this does not show

  • The face is invented by this project: the skull plus an assumed soft-tissue thickness, with parts that cannot be inferred from bone on purpose. A model trained on it learns that rule, not real faces.
  • It says nothing about forensic accuracy or identification, and nothing about how real faces vary with age, sex, ancestry or weight.
  • One prediction on one skull is an illustration. The tables above are the evidence, and they come with the caveat on sample size.
