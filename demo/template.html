UNCERTAFACE

A skull is found with parts missing. Build the face on it, show several plausible faces, and mark which regions are certain.

Read this first. The skulls are real, anonymised CT scans. The faces are synthetic: an assumed layer of soft tissue, not measured faces. This is not a forensic tool and it does not identify anyone.

Pale bone was given to the model; grey bone was hidden from it. The face is the model's real prediction ( points, interpolated onto a finer mesh for display). Certainty is the half-width of the calibrated 90 percent interval, per coordinate: each of the true point's left-right, front-back and up-down positions should lie within that many millimetres of the prediction about nine times in ten. How it works

1A real skull, partly hidden

Each training example hides 10 to 85 percent of the bone with realistic patterns: patches, a cap, only the top, only one side, or whole regions. The hidden fraction is measured on the surface, not assumed.

2Predict a face

A point-cloud network reads the visible bone and predicts the position of about 2,000 face points. A fixed template gives every skull the same vertex layout.

3Say how sure, and show the alternatives

For every point the model also predicts a spread in millimetres, checked and corrected on separate skulls (conformal calibration). From that spread the page draws four plausible faces and ranks the face regions from most to least certain.

4Check against the truth

The synthetic face is known exactly, so predicted certainty can be compared with real error on skulls kept out of training: by region and point by point.

What this does not show

  • The face is invented by this project: the skull plus an assumed soft-tissue thickness, with generic nose, lips, eyes and chin placed at assumed positions, and parts that cannot be inferred from bone on purpose. A model trained on it learns that rule, not real faces.
  • It says nothing about forensic accuracy or identification, and nothing about how real faces vary with age, sex, ancestry or weight.
  • One prediction on one skull is an illustration. The tables above are the evidence, and they come with the caveat on sample size.
